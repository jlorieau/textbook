## 1. What is a Function?

A function is a rule or mapping that takes one or more inputs and transforms them into one or more outputs. In physical sciences, this usually means taking one or more real numbers and generating a single output number.

```math
f: X \to Y, \quad y = f(x)
```

* **Independent variable ($x$)**: The quantity you control or vary directly (e.g., temperature, time, or the number of reactants).
* **Dependent variable ($y$)**: The quantity that responds to your inputs (e.g., reaction yield, kinetic energy, or pressure).

## 2. Multivariable Slices

When a function depends on multiple independent coordinates, such as $z = f(x, y)$, the function describes a **2D surface living in 3D space**:

```math
z = f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2
```

In physical chemistry and quantum mechanics, we frequently examine **2D cross-sections** by holding one variable constant (a partial derivative perspective):

```math
\left( \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} \right)_y
```

## 3. Domains of Validity: Classical vs. Relativistic Physics

A crucial error students make is assuming a scientific formula holds across all real numbers. Every physical equation has a strict **domain of validity**.

Consider the classical formula for kinetic energy:

```math
E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
```

In classical mechanics, $v$ can mathematically take any value in $[0, \infty)$. However, in relativistic physics, velocity is physically bounded by the speed of light $c$:

```math
E = \gamma m c^2 = \frac{m c^2}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}
```

As $v \to c$, the classical formula underpredicts the true energy by orders of magnitude. A function is only as valid as its domain!

  title="Real Domain of the Unit Circle"
  hint="Reorganize the circle equation to isolate y, and examine what values under the square root yield real numbers."
  solutionVideoUrl="https://youtu.be/KCFKsyrFnnw?t=15"
  solutionTimestampLabel="Starts at 0:15"
>
  Plot the function $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$ for a unit circle with $r = 1$ in the real domain. What are the constraints on the independent variable $x$?

  <div slot="solution">
    To plot with $y$ as the dependent variable:
    ```math
    y = \pm \sqrt{r^2 - x^2} = \pm \sqrt{1 - x^2}
    ```
    For $y$ to remain in the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$, the radicand must be non-negative:
    ```math
    1 - x^2 \ge 0 \implies x^2 \le 1 \implies -1 \le x \le 1
    ```
    For every input $x \in (-1, 1)$, there are two solutions ($+y$ and $-y$), reflecting symmetry about the $x$-axis.
  </div>
</ProblemDrawer>

  Express the unit circle ($r = 1$) in parametric form using angle $\theta$ as the independent variable.

  <div slot="solution">
    By introducing the parameter $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$:
    ```math
    \begin{cases}
      x(\theta) = \cos(\theta) \\
      y(\theta) = \sin(\theta)
    \end{cases}
    ```
    This parameterization converts a multi-valued relation into two single-valued functions, greatly simplifying integrals in polar coordinates.

## 1. Beyond Pre-Packaged Formulas

Calculus is the mathematics of how physical systems transform continuously. Rather than memorizing static formulas, calculus gives us the tools to derive equations from physical first principles.

### The Derivative as an Instantaneous Rate
For an arbitrary physical trajectory $x(t)$:

```math
v(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{x(t + \Delta t) - x(t)}{\Delta t} = \frac{dx}{dt}
```

### The Integral as Continuous Accumulation
Accumulating infinitesimal increments over a continuum:

```math
\Delta x = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v(t) \, dt
```

In quantum mechanics, differential operators act directly on state vectors:

```math
\hat{p}_x = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x}, \quad \hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + V(x)
```

## Practice Problem

<ProblemDrawer
  problemNumber="1"
  title="Differentiating a Gaussian Wave Packet"
  hint="Use the chain rule on the exponential power."
>
  Given the ground state wavefunction $\psi(x) = A e^{-\alpha x^2}$, find $\frac{d\psi}{dx}$ and determine where the probability density reaches its extremum.

  <div slot="solution">
    Applying the chain rule:
    ```math
    \frac{d\psi}{dx} = A \cdot (-2\alpha x) e^{-\alpha x^2} = -2\alpha x \psi(x)
    ```
    Setting $\frac{d\psi}{dx} = 0$ yields $x = 0$, confirming the probability distribution is centered at the origin.
  </div>
</ProblemDrawer>

## 1. Defining Internal Energy ($U$)

Internal energy $U$ represents the total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of all molecules in a thermodynamic system:

```math
U = E_{\text{trans}} + E_{\text{rot}} + E_{\text{vib}} + E_{\text{elec}} + E_{\text{intermolecular}}
```

For a monoatomic ideal gas, only translational degrees of freedom contribute:

```math
U = \frac{3}{2} n R T
```

---

## 2. Enthalpy ($H$) and Constant-Pressure Heat

When chemical reactions or biological processes occur at constant atmospheric pressure, systems expand or contract, doing work on the atmosphere:

```math
H = U + PV
```

Differentiating under constant pressure ($dP = 0$):

```math
dH = dU + P dV = (dq - P dV) + P dV = dq_P
```

Enthalpy change $\Delta H$ directly tracks the heat absorbed or released in open beakers and biological systems.

  title="Relating C_P and C_V for an Ideal Gas"
  hint="Differentiate H = U + nRT with respect to temperature."
>
  Derive Mayer's relation $C_P - C_V = nR$ for an ideal gas.

  <div slot="solution">
    Starting with $H = U + PV = U + nRT$:
    ```math
    \frac{dH}{dT} = \frac{dU}{dT} + nR
    ```
    Since $C_P = \left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial T}\right)_P$ and $C_V = \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V$:
    ```math
    C_P = C_V + nR \implies C_P - C_V = nR
    ```
  </div>
